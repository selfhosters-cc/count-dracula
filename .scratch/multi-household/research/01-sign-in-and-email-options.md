# Research: sign-in and email options

Ticket: [01 — Sign-in and email options](../issues/01-sign-in-and-email-options.md)
Checked: **2026-10-10**. Every price, quota and review time below was read on that date and can change.
Placeholder host: `count-dracula.example.com`.

## Bottom line

- **Everything below can be built with zero npm dependencies.** Bun 1.3+ ships argon2id/bcrypt password hashing, a cookie API wired into `Bun.serve`, CSRF tokens, and WebCrypto/`node:crypto` that verify ES256, RS256 and Ed25519 signatures. Every email provider here can be called with a plain `fetch`. The only things we'd have to write ourselves are a session table, rate limiting, and (for SES only) AWS request signing. (See §2 and §1.)
- **Realistic in about a week: email magic links** (one-time link sent by email, which also gives us email verification and account recovery), with sessions as a random token stored hashed in SQLite and kept in an HttpOnly cookie. The provider call is about 20 lines. The work is in the flow (single use, expiry, rate limits) and in DNS setup (SPF/DKIM/DMARC).
- **Passkeys are buildable without a library**, roughly 200–300 lines of server and client code by my estimate, using the browser's `getPublicKey()`/`getAuthenticatorData()` so we never parse CBOR. They don't remove the need for email: someone who loses their device still needs a recovery path. Better as a follow-up than as the only way in. (See §3.)
- **"Sign in with Google" fits the timeline.** It's free. Apps that request only `openid email profile` don't need Google's app verification. The ID token arrives directly from Google over HTTPS, so we don't need a JWT library. Without the optional brand verification (2–3 business days if it goes to manual review), the consent screen shows our domain instead of our app name and logo. **"Sign in with Apple"** costs **US$99/yr**, needs Apple Developer Program enrollment (Apple doesn't publish how long approval takes), an ES256 client-secret JWT that has to be re-signed at least every 6 months, and SPF-registered sending domains before we can email `privaterelay` addresses. It's a poor fit for Oct 13–23. (See §4.)
- **The email provider is the one real constraint.** Of the free tiers, **Resend** is the quickest to start: no account review is documented, the domain often verifies in about 15 minutes, and it's a plain HTTPS API. But the free plan caps sending at **100 emails/day**, which sign-up opening day (~Oct 25) could exceed. **Brevo** free allows **300/day**, but only after Brevo approves the account. **Amazon SES** costs pennies at our volume and has no daily cap that matters once out of the sandbox. Getting out takes a review AWS says gets a first response within 24 hours, and we'd hand-write about 50 lines of SigV4 signing. **Postmark's** free tier is **100/month**, too small. (See §1.)
- **Recommended route (for the grilling ticket to decide):** email magic links via Resend, with a plan for the 100/day cap: either pay **US$20 for one month of Resend Pro** across Oct 25–Nov 1, or apply for SES production access now as the backup. Keep sessions long (through Nov 1) so most households only ever need one email. Add Google sign-in only if the grill wants a no-email path. Leave passkeys and Apple until after Halloween. **Trade-offs:** magic links depend on email deliverability and on the inbox being on the same phone. Google adds a dependency on a third party plus a privacy-policy page. Passkeys are the most secure option but the most new code and support burden.

## 1. Email delivery from a homelab server

Sending straight from the homelab's own mail server isn't realistic. Gmail requires every sender to have valid forward **and reverse DNS (PTR)** for the sending IP, SPF or DKIM, TLS, and a spam rate below 0.3% ([Google email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)). Above **5,000 messages/day**, Gmail also requires SPF **and** DKIM **and** DMARC ([same page](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)). A relay provider handles the IP reputation and PTR side for us.

Bun has no built-in SMTP client. `Bun.connect` gives raw TCP with `tls: true`, which the Bun docs describe as "low-level, intended for library authors" ([Bun TCP docs](https://bun.com/docs/runtime/networking/tcp)). So "SMTP relay" would mean writing our own SMTP client. **Every provider below has an HTTPS API that works with a plain `fetch`, so that's the route to use.**

Notes per provider:

- **Resend.** Domains "often verify within 15 minutes", though DNS "can occasionally take up to 72 hours" ([add a domain](https://resend.com/docs/add-a-domain)). Resend recommends sending from a subdomain (e.g. `mail.count-dracula.example.com`) to isolate reputation ([domains](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/domains/introduction)). The API allows 10 requests/s per team. The daily quota applies only to the Free plan, resets at **midnight UTC**, and returns `429 daily_quota_exceeded` when exceeded ([rate limit](https://resend.com/docs/api-reference/rate-limit)). An `Idempotency-Key` header (valid 24h) stops double sends on retry ([send email](https://resend.com/docs/api-reference/emails/send-email)). The docs I checked describe no account review.
- **Postmark.** The developer tier only fits testing. Postmark also blocks signing up with a Gmail or Yahoo address ([Postmark blog](https://postmarkapp.com/blog/why-cant-i-use-gmail-address)).
- **Brevo.** The API rate limit (1,000 rps on all plans) isn't the constraint ([API limits](https://developers.brevo.com/docs/api-limits)). The constraint is the 300/day free cap after an approval step whose length Brevo doesn't publish ([pricing](https://www.brevo.com/pricing/)).
- **Amazon SES.** Cheapest per email. At 3,000 emails, the Essentials rate of $0.16/1,000 works out to about $0.48 ([pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/ses/pricing/)). There are two costs. One is the sandbox review: the request asks for a website URL, the mail type ("Transactional") and a bounce-handling acknowledgement ([request production access](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/request-production-access.html)). The other is that with no SDK we'd implement SigV4 ourselves: a canonical request, a chain of 4 HMAC-SHA256 calls to derive the signing key, and an `Authorization` header. AWS says to use an SDK "unless you have a good reason not to" ([SigV4](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/reference_sigv-create-signed-request.html)). HMAC-SHA256 is built into Bun (`node:crypto` `createHmac` and `Bun.CryptoHasher` ([Bun hashing](https://bun.com/docs/runtime/hashing))), so this is roughly 50 lines by my estimate, but they'd be untested lines written under time pressure.

**Volume check.** The ticket expects "a few hundred to a few thousand sign-ups in October". With about 1–2 emails per household (sign-up link, plus an occasional re-sign-in), Resend's 3,000/month free tier is enough in total. The **100/day** cap is the risk if sign-up opens around Oct 25 and more than ~50–100 households arrive on the same day. When the cap is hit, the API returns 429 until midnight UTC ([rate limit](https://resend.com/docs/api-reference/rate-limit)), which would leave new households unable to sign in.

**DNS summary.** Every provider needs DKIM on our domain. Resend, Postmark and SES also want an SPF/return-path record on a subdomain they control (MX/CNAME). DMARC isn't needed to verify with any provider here except Brevo, where it's part of the record set ([Brevo](https://developers.brevo.com/docs/domain-authentication-and-verification)). We stay well below Gmail's 5,000/day bulk threshold, so SPF *or* DKIM satisfies Gmail ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)). Adding a `p=none` DMARC record anyway is cheap.

## 2. Auth primitives already in Bun

Checked against the Bun docs (v1.4.3 docs site) and run on local **Bun 1.4.2** (probe script in the session scratchpad, not committed).

| Need | Built in? | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Password hashing | **Yes**: `Bun.password.hash/verify` (+ `hashSync`/`verifySync`) | Algorithms `argon2id` (**default**), `argon2i`, `argon2d`, `bcrypt`. The salt is generated and embedded automatically. `verify` detects the algorithm from the hash. With bcrypt, passwords over 72 bytes are pre-hashed with SHA-512 ([Bun hashing](https://bun.com/docs/runtime/hashing)). The defaults in source are argon2id with libsodium's "interactive" parameters and bcrypt cost 10 ([PasswordObject.rs](https://github.com/oven-sh/bun/blob/main/src/runtime/crypto/PasswordObject.rs)). Locally a default hash begins `$argon2id$v=19$m=65536,t=2,p=1`, i.e. 64 MiB of memory and 2 iterations. |
| Cookies | **Yes**: `Bun.Cookie`, `Bun.CookieMap`, and `req.cookies` in `Bun.serve` routes | Changes to `req.cookies` become `Set-Cookie` headers on the response automatically. Options include `httpOnly`, `secure`, `sameSite` (default `lax`), `maxAge`, `expires`, `path`, `domain`, `partitioned` ([Bun cookies](https://bun.com/docs/runtime/cookies)). Added in Bun v1.2.7 ([release notes](https://bun.com/blog/bun-v1.2.7)). Verified locally: `sid=abc; Path=/; Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax`. **Only works on `routes` handlers**, not a bare `fetch` handler ([Bun cookies](https://bun.com/docs/runtime/cookies)). |
| Sessions | **No** | The cookie docs cover reading and writing cookies, not sessions or cookie signing ([Bun cookies](https://bun.com/docs/runtime/cookies)). We'd hand-roll a `sessions` table in `bun:sqlite` (store the SHA-256 of a random token, look it up per request). That's small. |
| CSRF tokens | **Yes**: `Bun.CSRF.generate/verify` | HMAC-signed token with nonce and timestamp. Default lifetime 24h, `sha256`, `base64url`; can be bound to a `sessionId` ([Bun CSRF](https://bun.com/docs/runtime/csrf)). Added in Bun 1.3 ([release notes](https://bun.sh/blog/bun-v1.3)). |
| Random tokens | **Yes**: Web Crypto `crypto.getRandomValues`, `crypto.randomUUID`, `Bun.randomUUIDv7` | `randomUUIDv7` appears alongside `CookieMap` in the v1.2.7 notes ([release notes](https://bun.com/blog/bun-v1.2.7)). Verified present locally. |
| Hash / HMAC | **Yes**: `Bun.CryptoHasher` (sha256/512, blake2b, sha3 …, HMAC when a key is passed) and `node:crypto` | ([Bun hashing](https://bun.com/docs/runtime/hashing)) |
| Signature verification (passkeys, OIDC, Apple client secret) | **Yes**: WebCrypto `subtle` and `node:crypto` | Verified locally in Bun 1.4.2: ECDSA P-256, RSASSA-PKCS1-v1_5 and Ed25519 sign/verify. `node:crypto.verify` accepts a **DER** ECDSA signature against a **DER SPKI** key (`createPublicKey({format:"der",type:"spki"})`), which is exactly the format WebAuthn produces. |
| Constant-time compare | **Yes**: `node:crypto.timingSafeEqual` | Verified present locally. |
| CBOR decoding | **No** | `Bun.CBOR` is undefined locally. Only matters for passkeys, and §3 shows how to avoid it. |
| Rate limiting, email-sending, OAuth client, JWT library | **No** | Not in the Bun docs pages checked. Each is small enough to hand-write: a SQLite-backed counter, a `fetch` call, a redirect + `fetch`, and JWT encoding (header + payload + one `subtle.sign`). |

**Takeaway:** passwords, cookies, CSRF and crypto are covered by Bun. A session table, magic-link tokens and rate limiting are each a few dozen lines of our own code on `bun:sqlite`.

## 3. Passkeys / WebAuthn without a library

**Browser support.** `PublicKeyCredential` is Baseline "widely available" since September 2021 and needs HTTPS ([MDN WebAuthn](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Authentication_API), [MDN PublicKeyCredential](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/PublicKeyCredential)). Passkey autofill ("conditional mediation") is widely available since October 2023 ([MDN isConditionalMediationAvailable](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/PublicKeyCredential/isConditionalMediationAvailable_static)). `AuthenticatorAttestationResponse.getPublicKey()` is widely available since October 2023 ([MDN getPublicKey](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AuthenticatorAttestationResponse/getPublicKey)). The JSON helpers `parseCreationOptionsFromJSON()` and `toJSON()` are only **Baseline 2025, newly available** (since March 2025) ([MDN parseCreationOptionsFromJSON](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/PublicKeyCredential/parseCreationOptionsFromJSON_static), [MDN toJSON](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/PublicKeyCredential/toJSON)). So the client should carry its own ~15-line base64url conversion rather than depend on them, because household phones may be older.

**Spec status.** WebAuthn Level 3 is a W3C Recommendation dated 25 August 2026 ([WebAuthn L3](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/)).

**How much server code.** The spec's registration procedure (§7.1) has **32 numbered steps** and authentication (§7.2) has **29** ([WebAuthn L3 §7](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/#sctn-rp-operations)). Most are simple comparisons. The part that usually needs a library is decoding the CBOR attestation object and the COSE public key. **The spec provides a way around that**: when the Relying Party (the website the passkey belongs to) doesn't need attestation, the browser can do the parsing. `getPublicKey()` returns the key as DER SubjectPublicKeyInfo, `getPublicKeyAlgorithm()` returns its COSE algorithm, and `getAuthenticatorData()` returns the authenticator data "to remove the need to parse CBOR at all in many cases" ([WebAuthn L3 §5.2.1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/#sctn-public-key-easy)). Browsers **must** return a non-null key for **ES256 (-7), RS256 (-257) and Ed25519 (-8)** ([same section](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/#sctn-public-key-easy)). All three verify with Bun's built-in crypto (§2).

The no-library, no-attestation build:

1. **Register.** The server issues a random challenge of at least 16 bytes ([MDN WebAuthn](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Authentication_API)) with `attestation: "none"` and `pubKeyCredParams` of -7, -8 and -257. The client sends `clientDataJSON`, `getAuthenticatorData()`, `getPublicKey()` and `getPublicKeyAlgorithm()`. The server parses the JSON and checks `type === "webauthn.create"`, the challenge and the origin. It then reads `rpIdHash` (32 bytes, compared against SHA-256 of `count-dracula.example.com`), the flags byte (UP/UV), `signCount`, and the credential ID out of the authenticator data, and stores the SPKI, the algorithm and the credential ID. This skips the spec's CBOR step and the attestation-format steps, which the spec allows when "None attestation is acceptable under Relying Party policy" ([WebAuthn L3 §7.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/#sctn-registering-a-new-credential)).
2. **Sign in.** Check `type === "webauthn.get"`, the challenge, the origin, `rpIdHash`, the UP flag, and the BE/BS consistency. Then verify the signature over `authenticatorData ‖ SHA-256(clientDataJSON)` with the stored key, and handle `signCount` ([WebAuthn L3 §7.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/#sctn-verifying-assertion)). ECDSA signatures arrive DER-encoded; `node:crypto.verify` accepts them as they are (verified locally, §2).

**My estimate:** about 150–200 lines of server code (two binary parsers sharing an authenticator-data reader, plus challenge storage), about 60–100 lines of client code, plus tests. That's 2–3 focused days including testing on real iPhone and Android devices. **Caveats:**

- WebAuthn needs a secure context, so it only works on the real HTTPS hostname. The RP ID is tied to that hostname ([MDN WebAuthn](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Authentication_API)). Local testing needs `localhost` or the tailnet HTTPS host.
- A passkey alone gives no recovery path when a device is lost. We'd still need email or a printed recovery code.
- Several adults or phones per household means each one registers its own passkey, or we build an "invite a device" flow. That's product design for the household-identity ticket (04), not this one.

## 4. "Sign in with Google" / "Sign in with Apple"

### Google

- **Flow without a library.** This is the server-side OpenID Connect (OIDC) code flow. Redirect to `https://accounts.google.com/o/oauth2/v2/auth` with `scope=openid email`, `state` and `nonce`. On callback, check `state`, then `POST` the code with the client secret to `https://oauth2.googleapis.com/token` and read the `id_token` ([Google OIDC](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/openid-connect)). Because the token comes "directly with Google over an intermediary-free HTTPS channel and using your client secret", Google says "you can be confident that the token you receive really comes from Google and is valid". That means **no JWT signature verification** (no JWKS, no library), as long as the token isn't passed on to other components ([Google OIDC](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/openid-connect)). Then key the account on `sub`, not `email` (which can change), and check `email_verified` ([Google OIDC](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/openid-connect)). Redirect URIs must match exactly, and HTTP is refused ([Google OIDC](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/openid-connect)). My estimate is about 80–120 lines plus a Cloud Console setup.
- **App review.** "If your app utilizes only non-sensitive scopes, it is not mandatory for your app to complete the app verification process" ([OAuth app verification help](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463073?hl=en)). Showing our app name and logo on the consent screen needs the lighter **brand verification**. Without it, "only your application domain will be visible to users" ([Manage OAuth branding](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549049?hl=en)). Brand verification is usually automated in a few minutes, or **2–3 business days** if it goes to manual review, and a pass is valid for 7 days until you publish ([brand verification](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/brand-verification), [FAQ](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463817?hl=en)). Brand verification requires a public homepage and a **privacy policy on the same domain**, and the domain verified in **Google Search Console** ([brand verification](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/brand-verification)).
- **Publishing.** A project in "Testing" is limited to 100 listed test users; "Publish app" opens it to everyone ([Manage app audience](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945?hl=en)). Apps that request only name, email and profile are exempt from the 7-day expiry that applies to test users ([same page](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945?hl=en)).
- **Cost.** None of Google's OIDC or verification pages lists a fee for OAuth sign-in ([Google OIDC](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/openid-connect), [FAQ](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463817?hl=en)).
- **Fit:** **yes**, with an afternoon of console work. The privacy-policy page is needed for brand verification, and it overlaps with the map's "Privacy notice" open item.

### Apple

- **Cost and enrollment.** The Apple Developer Program is **US$99 per membership year** and lists Sign in with Apple as a member capability ([What's included](https://developer.apple.com/programs/whats-included/)). Individual enrollment needs an Apple Account with 2FA and a legal name. Organization enrollment needs a D-U-N-S number. **Apple doesn't publish an approval time** ([Enroll](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)).
- **Setup.** Create a **primary App ID** with Sign in with Apple enabled, then a **Services ID** grouped under it, listing our domains and return URLs. "You don't need to upload a file on your server" for domain registration ([Configure for the web](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/capabilities/configure-sign-in-with-apple-for-the-web/), [About Sign in with Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/capabilities/about-sign-in-with-apple/)).
- **Client secret.** An **ES256-signed JWT** (`kid`, `iss` = Team ID, `aud` = `https://appleid.apple.com`, `sub` = Services ID). Asking for an expiry more than **15,777,000 s (six months)** ahead is an error ([Creating a client secret](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accountorganizationaldatasharing/creating-a-client-secret)). We can sign it with WebCrypto ECDSA P-256 (§2), but it's another thing to rotate.
- **Flow quirks.** If you request the `name` or `email` scope, `response_mode` must be `form_post`, so Apple POSTs to our callback ([Incorporating into other platforms](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/signinwithapple/incorporating-sign-in-with-apple-into-other-platforms)). The user's name and email are shared only **the first time** the user signs in ([Authenticating users](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/signinwithapple/authenticating-users-with-sign-in-with-apple)).
- **Email interaction.** To email users who hide their address behind `@privaterelay.appleid.com`, we "must register [our] outbound emails or email domains and use … SPF". Each relay address has a **100 emails/day** limit ([Private email relay](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/signinwithapple/communicating-using-the-private-email-relay-service)).
- **Fit:** **poor for Oct 13–23.** It costs money, enrollment approval time is unpublished, and it brings App ID/Services ID/key/secret rotation plus relay-domain setup, all for a web-only app whose users can just as easily use email or Google.

## Routes at a glance (input for the grilling ticket)

| Route | New code (my estimate) | External setup / lead time | Recovery | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Email magic link (Resend) | ~150–250 lines: tokens, sessions, rate limits, templates | Domain DNS, often verified in ~15 min ([Resend](https://resend.com/docs/add-a-domain)) | Built in (it *is* email) | 100/day free cap ([pricing](https://resend.com/pricing)); emails landing in spam |
| Email magic link (SES) | Same + ~50 lines SigV4 | Sandbox review, first response within 24h ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/request-production-access.html)) | Built in | Review outcome; hand-rolled signing |
| Email + password | Same as magic link + `Bun.password` (built in) | Same as the chosen provider | Still needs reset-by-email | Password-reset flow and credential stuffing to handle |
| Passkeys (+ email for recovery) | +250–300 lines on top of email | None beyond HTTPS | Via email | New-device and multi-adult flows; testing on real devices |
| Google sign-in | ~80–120 lines | Console + privacy page; brand verification 2–3 business days, optional ([Google](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/brand-verification)) | Google's | Households without Google accounts still need another way in |
| Apple sign-in | ~120–180 lines incl. JWT signing | $99/yr + enrollment of unknown length ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)) | Apple's | Timeline and relay-email setup |

Not verified here, flagged for the build: corporate mail scanners and link previewers sometimes open GET links. A safe default is to have the magic link open a page whose **button** submits a POST to complete sign-in, so a prefetch can't use up the token.

Out of scope here (other tickets): bot and abuse controls on sign-up (03, 11), what a "household" identity is and how several adults share it (04), and the privacy notice (map, "Not yet specified").
